Cluj Hackathon 2026 ∙ Digital Romania ∙ 48 hours

Welcome!
We are thrilled to have you at Cluj Hackathon 2026. Over the next 48 hours you get to build a small piece of the
future of a digital Romania, a solution that can genuinely make people’s lives easier.
Let’s be honest: the state’s apps today are, in general, clunky, ugly and slow. Those are exactly what we want to
replace. So your challenge is not only to solve a problem, but to show that a public service can be fast, modern
and a pleasure to use.
This guide is here to help you throughout the competition. It is not a rigid rulebook, it is a map of what we are
looking for, what resources you have, and how to present your idea in its best light. Read it at ease, come back to
it whenever you need, and ask a mentor any time you get stuck.
One thing to keep in mind: you do not have to make everything perfect, you have to make something that matters.
Let’s get started.

What we really want
We want two things at once: a solution that solves a real problem and that looks wow.
Usefulness is the foundation, without it nothing else matters, and a shiny interface that does nothing concrete
will not help you. But we also do not want you to stop at “it works”. The design, the smoothness and the way the
product moves should impress from the very first touch. Especially since you have powerful tools at your disposal
(Lovable, Cursor, AI and the rest), there is no excuse for something that looks modest.
So aim high: solve a real problem and make it spectacular. Always ask yourselves: “Who are we helping, how do
we make their life simpler, and how do we make them say wow?”. If you have a clear answer, you are already on
the right track.
How to tell us your idea
When you present your project, it helps us a lot if you cover these four things. Think of them as a short story
about your solution:
1. What is it called? A clear, memorable name that says something about what the solution does.
2. What does it do, briefly? Two or three sentences anyone can understand: what it is, how it works and why
it is useful.
3. What problem does it solve? Tell us concretely which frustration in the relationship with the state disap‐
pears thanks to you: less bureaucracy, less wasted time, fewer trips.
4. What data do you use? Tell us which public data sources you use or, if you simulate data, what its structure
looks like.
That’s it. If you cover these four points, the jury will instantly understand the value of your work.
Where to get data
To anchor your solutions in the reality of Romania, we recommend starting from these official portals. You can
use the real data there or build simulated data modeled on them:
• data.gov.ro, the National Open Data Portal: datasets from ministries, agencies and official nomenclatures.
• insse.ro, the National Institute of Statistics: demographic, economic and territorial data.
• onrc.ro, the Trade Register: useful for entrepreneurship ideas (use simulated data based on its structure).
• domenii.gov.ro, the registry of government subdomains and resources.
And if you want inspiration from projects that already do things well, take a look at demoanaf.ro, pnd.robertslaboratory.com
and transparenta.eu. They are good examples of what a pleasant digital interaction with the state can look like.

A few technical things that make your solution solid
Because we are dealing with citizens’ data, there are a few aspects that matter a lot and that the jury will appreciate.
Do not be scared, they are more like common‐sense principles that we explain here:
Data is not lost and not falsified. Everything related to history, transactions, status changes or issued documents
should be impossible to delete or quietly modify. You can use blockchain technology or, more simply, a valid
simulation of a distributed ledger. The basic idea: whatever happened stays recorded and transparent.
Data is protected and access is controlled. Information should be encrypted, and only the right people should
reach it:
• The citizen, the rightful owner of the data, with full control.
• The appropriate civil servant, only within their area of competence and only while handling a request.
• Other authorized systems, through secure connections.
Authentication like in real life (ROeID simulation). There is no public API for the official digital identity, so please
simulate it:
• a login flow based on credentials;
• a two‐factor (2FA) step, meaning a code via SMS, e‐mail, an Authenticator‐type app or a push notification,
before the user enters the account.
The solution is for everyone (accessibility). Because it is a public service, it should be easy to use including for
people with disabilities (the European standard recommends a minimum level of AA). In practice: full keyboard
navigation, screen reader support (ARIA attributes, semantic HTML), and legible text with good contrast (at least
4.5:1).
Stack and design? Your choice
Here you have total freedom. We do not impose any programming language, framework or design system. Use
the technologies you feel at home with and that best serve your idea.
If you still want a starting point, many teams are comfortable with React / Next.js / Tailwind on the frontend,
Supabase / PostgreSQL on the backend, n8n or Temporal for automation, and OpenAI / AI agents / MCP for the
AI part. A coherent component set (ShadCN, for example) and a single icon family help the look stay tidy, but only
if it suits you. You decide.
One piece of advice from us: make the solution look good on phone, tablet and desktop. People use public
services mostly from their phones.
Tools you have access to
To build faster and better, you have access to a set of powerful tools. Use them as much and however you like,
they can save you hours of work:
• Lovable: builds web apps fast, starting from natural‐language descriptions. Great for spinning up a working
prototype in minutes.
• Cursor: a code editor with built‐in AI that helps you write, understand and fix code much faster.
• n8n: visual automation and workflows; connect services and APIs and put processes on autopilot, with
minimal code.
• ElevenLabs: realistic AI voice (text‐to‐speech). Useful for voice assistants, accessibility or in‐app audio
guidance.
• Anthropic Claude: a powerful AI model for text, good for conversational assistants, document processing,
summaries and smart form filling.

Design and the way it moves
This is where a lot is won or lost. If the state’s apps today are slow and frustrating, your solution should be the
exact opposite: an experience that flows. We are not talking about decorations, but about how the product feels
in your hand. Here is what to watch:
• Speed, above all. Everything must respond instantly. Screens load fast, buttons react on the spot, nothing
freezes. If an action takes time, show a clear indicator (skeleton, spinner, progress), never a dead screen.
• Smooth transitions between screens. Moves between pages and states should be smooth, not abrupt. A
short animation (150‐300 ms) that shows where the user comes from and goes to makes everything feel
coherent and considered.
• Microinteractions with a purpose. A button that confirms the tap, a field that validates as you type, a check
that appears when you finish a step. These small details create a sense of quality and control.
• Instant, clear feedback. The user must always know what is happening: did it work? did it fail? is it loading?
Short, friendly messages, no cryptic error codes.
• Clean, modern design. Generous spacing, clear visual hierarchy, legible typography, a coherent palette. Less
clutter means easier to use. Let the content breathe.
• Smoothness on any screen. The same sense of speed on a grandparent’s phone and on a civil servant’s
desktop. Optimize images, avoid unnecessary loads, think mobile‐first.
A good test: hand the phone to someone who has never seen the app. If they move through it on their own, with
pleasure and without getting stuck, you have won half the competition.
Who you are building for
For the citizen. Think about every screen, every button and every word from the perspective of an ordinary,
hurried person who does not want opaque bureaucratic jargon. If your mother or grandfather could use the
solution without asking anyone “and now what do I press?”, you have won.
Getting ready to be judged
Near the end, there are a few things that turn a prototype into a serious project:
• Put the solution online. A live link (GitHub Pages, Vercel, Netlify or whatever you prefer) works wonders:
the jury wants to be able to touch it, not just see it in slides. Attach the URL in the description.
• Tidy code. Clean code, split into clear components, is easier for mentors and judges to follow.
• A clear README. Say which libraries you used and why, and comment the important functions (the 2FA
simulation, the data part, the security logic).

How we score you
We want it to be transparent, so here is exactly how judging works. There are two stages:
1. During the hackathon, mentor teams rotate through all teams and score progress. The top 10 teams ad‐
vance to the Finals.
2. The final jury (Final Pitch), where the top 10 teams present their solution and the jury decides the winners.

Notice how much UX / Usability weighs: 25 points, on par with social impact. That is why design and the way

A short checklist before you submit
□ We can explain in two sentences who we help and how.
□ The four presentation points are covered (name, what it does, what problem, what data).
□ We have login + 2FA simulated.
□ Important data is impossible to delete/modify (blockchain or simulated ledger).
□ Access to data is controlled (citizen / civil servant / systems).
□ The solution is easy to use, including by keyboard, with good contrast.
□ It moves fast and smooth: instant response, fine transitions, clear feedback.
□ Modern, clean design that looks better than the state’s apps today.
□ It works nicely on phone, tablet and desktop.
□ We have a live link and a clear README.
Good luck and have fun! We can’t wait to see what you build.
